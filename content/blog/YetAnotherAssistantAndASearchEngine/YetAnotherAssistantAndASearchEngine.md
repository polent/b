## Yet Another Assistant and a Search Engine?

Once again, major companies like Google and OpenAI roll out their latest offerings. Google introduces Pixie, a new assistant. OpenAI follows with a brand new search engine. I am not as excited as one might expect.

### Google Pixie

Google's upcoming Pixel 9 smartphones are expected to introduce a new AI assistant named Pixie. It runs on Google's Gemini large language model. Pixie aims to be more personal than the current Google Assistant. It uses data from Google services like Maps and Gmail to perform complex and multimodal tasks. For example, Pixie could analyze a product in a photo and provide directions to the nearest store where it can be purchased.

The Pixel 9 series, including the Pixel 9 and Pixel 9 Pro, is set to get features like Adaptive Touch for better touch sensitivity. The focus is on user interaction and AI integration. The devices are expected to use advanced AI to stand out from other Android devices. Pixie will be exclusive to Google's hardware.

### OpenAI Search

OpenAI is reportedly preparing to launch its own AI-powered search engine. It is expected to be a strong competitor to Google search. It will likely be integrated with ChatGPT. That combines generative AI with conventional web search. The search engine could then show AI-generated summaries next to traditional search results. The goal is more relevant answers that fit the context.

The user interface is expected to be adaptive. On desktop, AI summaries and search results show side by side. On mobile, they might appear one after the other. This could improve accessibility and usability across devices. It should work the same on a phone or a computer.

## Do Assistants Solve a Real Problem?

It's a familiar scenario. Companies claim their smart assistants will simplify our lives. But these gadgets often end up as expensive ornaments that seem more interested in our personal details than in genuinely assisting us.

## OpenAI's Search Engine: Just Another Data Issue?

OpenAI steps into the market with its new AI-driven search engine. I am skeptical about its true intent. With the power of ChatGPT, this platform could simply become another tool for data collection. It might not be a provider of unbiased search results at all. Its debut is set to challenge established giants like Google. That raises concerns about privacy and about [manipulative algorithms](/blog/UniversalCommerceProtocolSoundsOpenItIsNot/) that put profit over user benefit.

The search engine promises better user interactions through AI-generated content. It remains to be seen whether it will respect user privacy. It may also follow existing platforms that harvest user data. The tech community and consumers are watching closely. I may hope for a tool that prioritizes transparency and fairness in the age of information overload. But I may be wrong.

## Big Tech's Profit Machine

These announcements are hardly surprising. Tech giants continuously churn out new products. They do it to line their pockets, not to better the world. Each new assistant and search engine seems to be another way to [harvest and monetize our data](/blog/TrackingLike/).

## Time for a Tech Reality Check

We face a massive stream of new gadgets and search engines. It's important to reflect on their real necessity and impact. It is time to back technologies that keep the internet an open platform. The internet must stay a freely accessible resource, not a luxury marketplace.

We must push for a web that stays a broad platform for innovation and knowledge sharing. It should not become just a data-mining field for profit. It's important to support technologies that respect user privacy and offer content freely. They should not turn our digital spaces into high-cost environments. Sustainability is also a reason for this. We deserve a web that puts our needs as users first, not our value as data sources. The internet should stay open and accessible for everyone.
